1. Background and Problem

At its Blue Hub facility, CEVA Logistics packs cosmetics into small kit boxes in two styles and 16 sizes, and the box can change with every order [1]. The SIT-CEVA Joint Lab plans to automate this line in seven work packages (Figure 1) [2]. This project covers the two stations on either side of kitting: WP2 forms the empty box and WP4 seals it after filling. Both steps are manual today, with six operators folding boxes and three sealing them [3], and CEVA names faster SKU changeover as a core challenge [2].

2. Value and Novelty

CEVA budgets SGD 100,000 for WP2 and SGD 150,000 for WP4, each expected to save about one operator in the first year and 2.5 by the third [2]. Existing case erectors and seal labellers are stand-alone machines, built for long runs of one product and adjusted by hand between formats (WP4 Section 2.2). Our novelty is that the two stations work as one connected system: one SKU recipe, selected once, sets up both machines without manual adjustment (Section 4), and both are built to be integrated into CEVA's full kitting line.

3. Shared Requirements

The two stations' Research Collaboration Arrangements (RCAs) share their main targets: 15 boxes per minute, at least 99.8 % success over an 8-hour, 7,200-box run across both stations, a 1000 x 1200 mm footprint each, and recipe selection on an HMI [4, 5]. The full WP4 list is in Appendix A of the WP4 page. Both stations handle the same 16 boxes, 11.5 to 20 cm long, 6 to 24 cm wide and 3.5 to 11.5 cm tall (Figure 2; dimensions in Appendix A below).

4. Shared Approach: Recipe-Driven Changeover

Each station turns the selected recipe's box dimensions into positions for its motorised axes (Figure 3), so changing SKU means picking a recipe, not changing tools.

5. Scope and Plan

Within CDE4301, we deliver a proof of concept of each station's critical functions, tested against the RCA targets. Kitting staff close the boxes between our stations [3]. The data links to CEVA's systems (WP4 UAT-13 and UAT-16) depend on ET1, a separate work package [2], so they follow after CDE4301. All hands-on work runs under approved Activity Risk Assessments [6]. The plan (Figure 4) covers RCA milestone M1 and the laboratory part of M2; deployment at Blue Hub follows after the course (Appendix B).

Appendix B: Joint Milestones and the CEVA Programme

The RCAs set three milestones for each station [4, 5]: M1, data collection and design concept, at T0 + 6 months; M2, purchase, integration, testing and deployment at Blue Hub, at T0 + 9 months; and completion at T0 + 12 months. CDE4301 covers M1 and the laboratory part of M2. CEVA's programme places WP2's site acceptance test at about month 12 and WP4's at about month 18 [2], so our proofs of concept feed into those tests rather than replace them.
